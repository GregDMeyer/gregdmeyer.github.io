Sourdough pizza dough

Published

July 31, 2026

The ingredient amounts listed below are per pizza. This recipe makes 12” pizzas (although you can stretch them out to more like 16” with a thinner crust).

How many pizzas should I make?

If you’re making pizza for \(k\) people, I’d recommend making anywhere from \(n = \frac{2}{3} k\) pizzas (if you will have a bunch of side dishes, people aren’t hungry, and you don’t want leftovers) to \(n = k\) pizzas (if everyone is getting back from a hike starving and you won’t have sides).

Sorry, you’re reading the recipe of a nerd, in the recipe below I’m gonna use \(n\) as the number of pizzas.

The process

9 PM the night before: feed starter

Tomorrow you will need 50\(n\) grams of starter. So, mix together in a bowl:

  • 25\(n\) g water
  • 10\(n\) g whole wheat flour
  • 15\(n\) g white flour
  • some sourdough starter (maybe a few spoonfuls)

Mix it all up and then put it on the counter, covered.

Tips
  • I use a pot lid to cover it. You don’t want it to dry out on top.
  • It will roughly double in size (or more) overnight, so make sure the bowl is big enough!
  • Ideally the amount of starter left over tomorrow will equal the amount of old starter you put in. Sometimes I make a little extra today to account for losing a little stuck to the bowl etc.
  • The ratio of whole wheat to white flour is not really important. You can even just use white flour sometimes. But somehow I think the yeast and bacteria like the nutrition of some whole wheat. I would not use only whole wheat because you will start to really taste it in the pizza.

Noon (day of): autolyse

This step allows the gluten to develop a bit before the yeast and other critters in the starter start munching on it.

Mix together in a bowl:

  • 135\(n\) g water
  • 50\(n\) g “golden wheat/white whole wheat” flour
  • 150\(n\) g bread flour

Let it sit for 20-30 mins. now. Because of ADHD I have sometimes let it sit for like an hour and a half. It was fine.

Tips
  • You can use all-purpose or “00” pizza flour instead of bread flour; the former will lead to a less glutinous dough and the latter more. Ultimately it will affect how “chewy” the pizza is.
  • You can replace the golden wheat flour with white flour if you want. But I really like golden wheat a lot—it makes the pizza more hearty and nutritious without making it taste like whole wheat.
  • Once the ingredients combine it will be pretty stiff; a stand mixer with a dough hook is nice but you can also just do it by hand. I have broken wooden spoons this way.

Half an hour later: add salt and starter

You should find that now the dough is much more coherent and stretchy, because the gluten has developed over the past half hour.

Add into the dough:

  • 50\(n\) g sourdough starter
  • \(\frac{1}{2} n\) teaspoons salt (maybe a bit more)

Adding the starter to the dough is kind of annoying because it’s slimy and slippery, and the now-stretchy dough is firm. I like to stretch out the dough on the counter (or inside the bowl of a stand mixer), put the starter on top, and then fold the edges of the dough into the middle to make a little pouch with the starter inside. Then I use the stand mixer with the dough hook, or repeatedly stretch out and fold over the pouch on the counter by hand, until the dough is more or less a homogenous consistency.

Cover the dough in a bowl and let sit for an hour or so.

Warning

There should be a little extra starter left over after you have added the right amount to the dough. Before you do anything else, put it into a jar and into the fridge! It’s now all fed and happy to hang out until you make pizza or bread next time.

If you don’t do this right away someone will try to be helpful and clean up the kitchen, and they will think that the last little bit of sourdough starter in the bowl is to be discarded and then you won’t have any sourdough starter any more. (Can you tell that this happened to me).

Tips
  • The starter should be well-incorporated in the dough, but the result doesn’t have to be totally smooth. A little lumpy is fine for now.

1 hour later: stretch and fold

After an hour, do a stretch and fold on the dough.

I typically use a flexible dough scraper to pull the dough out of the bowl and onto the counter for this.

Put the dough back in the bowl, cover, and let sit for another hour.

Note

Do not skip this! When I was learning I was like “this step is probably not actually important.” It is SO important. It’s what develops the gluten into a strong dough.

1 hour later: another stretch and fold

Do it again!

It should be roughly 3 PM by now.

Cover the dough in a bowl again, and let it rise.

1 hour before baking

Now you start cooking!

Form dough balls

Pull the dough out of the bowl onto a floured countertop, and use a dough scraper (if you have one) to divide it into \(n\) equal-size parts. Form them into dough balls, and try to do a good job developing a tight skin on the dough balls.

Try not to make any gigantic holes/bubbles, but also try not to squish out all the beautiful bubbles that have formed in the dough during fermentation.

Put the dough balls on the counter near where you will form them into pizzas, and cover them, maybe with a damp cloth, so that they don’t dry out while they are sitting there.

Pre-heat the oven

You want your pizza stone/steel to get all the way up to temp, and they hold a lot of heat. So put your pizza stone in the oven and start pre-heating it early.

If I’m using a regular oven, I just set it to the highest temperature it will go to (typically 550F). If you’re using an actual pizza oven, I let it preheat until its maybe 700F in the middle of the pizza stone.

Make the pizzas!

Once the oven is pre-heated and the dough balls have had a chance to sit for a while, start making pizza!

Note

If you don’t wait long enough after forming the dough balls, the gluten will be too tight and it will be hard to stretch the dough out flat enough (it will just bounce back). It will be especially tight if you use high-protein flour, or omit the golden wheat flour. It also depends on how warm your kitchen is. If it’s too tight, just give it a little more time to relax.

Notes about timing

If you start late (say 2 PM)

Reduce the time before the stretch-and-fold steps to 45 mins. There will be less time for the dough to rise before you bake, but the nice thing about pizza is that it’s still yummy even if the dough is not that bubbly.

Rising in the fridge overnight

On the other hand, you can shift the whole timeline earlier by 12-24 hours, and then after the second stretch and fold, put the dough in the fridge. Pull it out ~2 hours before you are ready to use it, and let it warm up on the counter.

Doing this makes the sour flavor of the dough develop more, it’s really tasty!
